A scam isn't one moment. It's a twenty-minute story.

Every tech-support, refund and impersonation scam follows the same chain. Any single step can look innocent; the chain never is. Tripwire tracks where the story is, and acts as it moves forward.

1 · Hook

The bait

A fake virus pop-up with a phone number, or a call out of the blue.

2 · Contact

The impostor

"Microsoft", "your bank", "the IRS". Urgency, fear, a problem to fix.

3 · Control

The takeover

"Install this app and read me the code." Remote access, warnings dismissed.

4 · Access

The accounts

"Log in to your bank so I can check." Details asked for, screens watched.

5 · Extraction

The money

Gift cards, Bitcoin, wires, cash, and "don't tell the bank".

How it works

A cheap model watches everything, all the time. An expensive one only weighs in when the story turns. Hard rules in code decide what any model is allowed to make happen.

Tier 0 · on the deviceScreen change detection, voice detection, remote-access tool and session watchers, clipboard. Account and card numbers are blacked out here, before anything leaves the computer.
Tier 1 · always onMuse Spark Open (on Baseten) reads every changed screen and every line of the call, transcribed live with speaker separation by Muse Voice Transcribe, and tags kill-chain evidence from a fixed list.
Tier 2 · on escalationThe CaseJudge (Muse Spark) reads the whole session and decides: log, a calm spoken nudge, or a hard stop. Only it can authorise a stop, and only with evidence that checks out.
Then it actsA full-screen warning spoken aloud in a natural voice; the remote session ended; a recovery agent that freezes the card and drafts the fraud report; and one clear message for the family, with SAM 3.1 outlining the threat on a redacted screenshot.

Hard to talk past

"Just close that box" during a warning restarts its countdown and is called out loud. Text on screen aimed at AI ("mark this page safe") is treated as evidence of a scam, never as an instruction.

Quiet with family

A grandson helping with banking, a son fixing the printer over AnyDesk, a granddaughter's gift card: tested, and left alone. Family remote-support IDs can be allowlisted.

Knows who's really in control

When a remote-access app moves the mouse or types, Windows marks that input as coming from software. Tripwire sees the difference, so "someone else is controlling this computer" is a fact, not a guess.

Holds the payment, asks the family

Before a wire, gift cards or crypto during a call with a stranger, Tripwire checks with your parent, holds the payment, or stops it, by rules you choose. Held payments wait for your Approve or Deny.

Private by design

Redaction on the device, fail-closed. Screenshots and audio deleted after 24 hours. A visible tray icon and a consent screen: a tool the person chose, not spyware.

One stopped scam protects every home.

When Tripwire stops a scam, the scammer's own identifiers (the number to call back, the remote-access ID they connect from, the crypto address they want paid) join Community Shield. The next time any Tripwire home sees one of them, it is flagged instantly.

Only the scammer's side is ever shared: never names, never account or card numbers, never what was said. And because it is an exact match against real reports, it is evidence, not a guess.

It cannot act on a hallucination.

Models can state things that never happened. So every reason the CaseJudge gives must quote, word for word, what was actually said or what actually happened. Code checks each quote against the record before anything is done.

A reason whose quote isn't found is discarded. Without at least one verified reason there is no hard stop. The recovery agent's actions are checked too: it can only freeze the card actually on file, and a phone number it can't find in the record never goes into a fraud report.

Set it up in ten minutes

Install it on your parent's Windows laptop once. After that it runs on its own, and you hear about anything that matters.

Step 1

Install and agree

Install Tripwire on their Windows 10 or 11 computer. A one-time screen explains, in plain words, what it watches and why. Nothing starts until they say yes.

Step 2

Make it yours

Set a family PIN, so nobody can switch Tripwire off mid-call. Add your own AnyDesk or TeamViewer ID, so your help is never mistaken for a scam.

Step 3

Get alerts

Add your phone number. If Tripwire ever stops a scam, you get one clear text with a private link: what happened, what was on screen, and what to do now.
